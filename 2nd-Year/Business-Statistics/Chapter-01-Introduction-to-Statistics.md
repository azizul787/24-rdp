## Part A — Easy Explanation (Story Style)

</div>

### Imagine this scene…

> Your school is having a **sports day** next week.
> The headmaster needs to know:
> - How many students will join?
> - Which class has more boys, which has more girls?
> - How many medals to order?
> - How much food to cook?

To get the answers, every class teacher writes a list and sends it to the headmaster. Now the headmaster has many **numbers**. He puts them in a clean table, finds totals, draws a chart, and finally takes his decisions.

**This whole work is called STATISTICS.**

> [!TIP]
> **One-line memory:**
> **Statistics = working with numbers to take a good decision.**

### Two meanings of the word "Statistics"

| Sense | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Plural** | The numbers (data) themselves | "The statistics of population is 17 crore" |
| **Singular** | The science / subject | "I am studying Statistics in BBA" |

### Q1. Define Statistics. Discuss the nature / characteristics of Statistics. &nbsp; `[Marks: 10]`

#### Introduction
In modern business, government, and research, no decision can be taken correctly only by guess. Every good decision needs facts, and facts come from numbers. **Statistics** is the science that deals with these numbers. In the field of business administration, statistics is now the most powerful tool for taking right decisions at the right time.

#### Origin of the Word

| Language | Word | Meaning |
|----------|------|---------|
| Latin | *Status* | Political state |
| Italian | *Statista* | Statesman |
| German | *Statistik* | Political state |
| French | *Statistique* | Political state |

In olden days, kings used statistics to count people, soldiers, lands and wealth. So statistics was first known as the **"science of kings"** or **"science of state."**

#### Meaning of Statistics
The word *Statistics* is used in two senses:

1. **Plural sense (Statistics as Data)** — Numerical facts collected for any purpose.
   *Example:* Population of Bangladesh, GDP, NU exam result.
2. **Singular sense (Statistics as Science / Method)** — The science which deals with the **collection, organization, presentation, analysis, and interpretation** of numerical data.

#### Definitions of Statistics

> **Prof. Horace Secrist:**
> "Statistics may be defined as the aggregate of facts affected to a marked extent by multiplicity of causes, numerically expressed, enumerated or estimated according to reasonable standards of accuracy, collected in a systematic manner for a predetermined purpose, and placed in relation to each other."

> **Croxton and Cowden:**
> "Statistics may be defined as the science which deals with collection, presentation, analysis and interpretation of numerical data."

> **A. L. Bowley:**
> "Statistics is the science of counting." Again — "Statistics is the science of averages."

> **Prof. R. A. Fisher:**
> "The science of statistics is essentially a branch of applied mathematics and may be regarded as mathematics applied to observational data."

> [!TIP]
> **Exam tip:** Write **at least 2** definitions. Croxton & Cowden is the easiest to memorize — write that one for sure.

#### Nature / Characteristics of Statistics (8 Points)

| # | Point | Easy meaning |
|---|-------|--------------|
| 1 | **Aggregate of facts** | A group of facts, not a single fact |
| 2 | **Numerically expressed** | Only numbers; not feelings or quality |
| 3 | **Affected by many causes** | Many reasons together create the data |
| 4 | **Reasonable accuracy** | Not 100% perfect, but close enough |
| 5 | **Systematically collected** | With a proper plan |
| 6 | **Predetermined purpose** | Collected for a specific goal |
| 7 | **Placed in relation** | Comparable with each other |
| 8 | **Enumerated or estimated** | Counted directly OR found from sample |

> **Detailed explanation (write in exam):**
>
> 1. **Aggregate of facts:** Mark of one student is *not* statistics; marks of all students of a class is statistics.
> 2. **Numerically expressed:** Beauty, intelligence, honesty are *not* statistics — unless converted into marks.
> 3. **Affected by multiplicity of causes:** Price of rice depends on production, demand, weather, transport, government policy.
> 4. **Reasonable standard of accuracy:** Data must not mislead the reader.
> 5. **Collected systematically:** With proper plan, otherwise the result is wrong.
> 6. **Predetermined purpose:** Without a purpose, data has no value.
> 7. **Placed in relation to each other:** We cannot compare student height with rice price.
> 8. **Enumerated or estimated:** When full counting is impossible, we use a sample.

#### Conclusion
From the above discussion we can say that Statistics is both a **science** and an **art** — science because of its systematic methods, art because it solves real-life problems skillfully. In modern times, no business, no government, and no research can run a single day without statistics.

### Q2. Discuss the functions of Statistics. &nbsp; `[Marks: 10]`

#### Introduction
Statistics is a very useful science. It helps a businessman, a government officer, a teacher and a researcher to take right decisions. To do so, statistics performs many important functions. The main functions are discussed below.

#### Functions of Statistics (9 Points)

| # | Function | One-line meaning |
|---|----------|------------------|
| 1 | Presents facts in a definite form | "Many passed" → "85% passed" |
| 2 | Simplifies mass of data | Big data → small averages, charts |
| 3 | Helps in comparison | This year vs last year |
| 4 | Studies relationship | Advertisement ↔ Sales |
| 5 | Helps in formulation of policies | Budget, business plan |
| 6 | Helps in forecasting | Predict future demand |
| 7 | Helps in testing hypothesis | Right or wrong claim |
| 8 | Enlarges knowledge | Know facts of whole world |
| 9 | Measures uncertainty | Risk through probability |

> **Detailed write-up for exam:**
>
> 1. **Presents facts in a definite form:** Statistics gives exact numerical statements. *"Many students passed"* is vague but *"85% students passed"* is definite.
> 2. **Simplifies mass of data:** A big and complex data set is hard to read. Statistics simplifies it by averages, percentages, ratios, tables, and graphs.
> 3. **Helps in comparison:** We can compare this year's sales with last year's sales, one student's marks with another, one country's GDP with another.
> 4. **Studies relationship:** Through correlation and regression, statistics shows the relationship between two variables (e.g., advertisement cost and sales).
> 5. **Helps in policy making:** Government and businesses make policies based on statistical data. Example: Bangladesh national budget.
> 6. **Helps in forecasting:** By time series analysis we predict future demand, future sales, future prices.
> 7. **Helps in testing hypothesis:** Through t-test, chi-square test we check whether a statement is true.
> 8. **Enlarges knowledge:** A single person can know facts of a whole country only because of statistics.
> 9. **Measures uncertainty:** Through probability, statistics measures risk and chance.

#### Conclusion
From the above, it is clear that statistics is the **backbone of every business, government and research**. Without statistics, decision making would be only a matter of guess.

### Q3. Discuss the importance / scope / uses of Statistics in business. &nbsp; `[Marks: 10]`

#### Introduction
In modern business, every step depends on data. From buying raw materials to selling final products, every decision needs numerical facts. So Statistics has become a must-needed tool for every businessman.

#### Importance of Statistics in Business (9 Areas)

| Area | How Statistics is used |
|------|------------------------|
| **Marketing** | Find popular products, demand area, advertisement cost, pricing |
| **Production** | Quality control, target setting, reduce wastage |
| **Finance & Accounting** | Profit-loss analysis, ratio analysis, budgeting |
| **Human Resource** | Employee performance, salary fixing, absenteeism rate |
| **Banking & Insurance** | Risk of loan, premium calculation, life-tables |
| **Investment** | Market trend, share price movement, risk before investing |
| **Auditing** | Sampling method to check accounts of big companies |
| **Research & Development** | Test new products before launching |
| **Management Control** | Compare actual performance with target |

> **Detailed write-up:**
>
> 1. **In Marketing** — Statistics helps to know which product is popular, which area has more demand, how much advertisement is needed, and how to fix the price.
> 2. **In Production / Operation** — Statistics helps to control quality, fix production target, reduce wastage and check machine performance.
> 3. **In Finance and Accounting** — Statistics helps in profit-loss analysis, ratio analysis, capital budgeting and forecasting income.
> 4. **In Human Resource Management** — Statistics measures employee performance, fixes salary and finds absenteeism rate.
> 5. **In Banking and Insurance** — Banks measure loan risk; insurance companies fix premium using probability.
> 6. **In Investment** — Investors study market trend, share price and risk before investing.
> 7. **In Auditing** — Auditors use sampling because checking every transaction of big companies is impossible.
> 8. **In R&D** — New products and ideas are tested by statistical methods before launch.
> 9. **In Management Control** — Top management compares actual performance with target and takes corrective action.

#### Conclusion
From the above, statistics is the **lifeblood of modern business**. Every department — from marketing to finance — depends on statistics for wise and profitable decisions.

### Q4. What are the limitations of Statistics? Why do people sometimes distrust statistics? &nbsp; `[Marks: 10]`

#### Introduction
Although Statistics is a very useful science, it is not free from limitations. If used carelessly or dishonestly, it gives wrong results. That is why some people distrust statistics. The limitations and reasons of distrust are given below.

#### Limitations of Statistics (7 Points)

1. **Does not study individual items** — Statistics deals only with aggregates.
2. **Deals only with quantitative data** — Beauty, honesty cannot be studied directly.
3. **Results are true only on average** — Not 100% true for every single case.
4. **Can be misused** — *"Like a useful medicine — used by a good doctor it cures, used by a wrong person it kills."*
5. **Requires expert knowledge** — Without proper knowledge, results go wrong.
6. **Only one of the methods** — Sometimes other methods are also needed.
7. **Statistical laws are not exact** — Based on probability, not certainty.

#### Reasons for Distrust of Statistics

> **Famous quote (must write):** *"There are three kinds of lies — lies, damned lies, and statistics."* — **Disraeli**

| # | Reason |
|---|--------|
| 1 | Use of false data by dishonest persons |
| 2 | Wrong interpretation by people who do not know statistics |
| 3 | Selection of biased samples |
| 4 | Unfair comparison of two unrelated things |
| 5 | Hiding important information |

#### Conclusion
So we can say statistics is not at fault — the fault is with the user. As **Prof. W. I. King** said, *"Statistics are like clay of which one can make a god or a devil as one pleases."* If used honestly, statistics gives the best result.

### Q5. Distinguish between Population and Sample. &nbsp; `[Marks: 5]`

#### Definitions
- **Population (Universe):** The total group of items / persons under study. *Example:* All students of NU.
- **Sample:** A small part selected from the population. *Example:* 100 students from NU for a survey.

#### Differences

| # | Point | Population | Sample |
|---|-------|-----------|--------|
| 1 | Meaning | Whole group | Part of group |
| 2 | Size | Large | Small |
| 3 | Mean symbol | μ (mu) | x̄ (x-bar) |
| 4 | SD symbol | σ (sigma) | s |
| 5 | Cost & Time | More | Less |
| 6 | Method | Census | Sampling |
| 7 | Accuracy | More accurate | Less but reliable |
| 8 | Example | All factory workers | 50 workers surveyed |

#### Conclusion
When the population is small, we study the whole population. When it is very large, we use a sample to save time and cost.

### Q6. What is Variable and Attribute? &nbsp; `[Marks: 5]`

#### Variable
A characteristic that can be **measured in numbers** and changes from one item to another.
*Examples:* height, weight, age, income, marks.

> **Two types of variable:**
> - **Discrete:** Whole-number values only (e.g. number of students = 20, 21, 22 — never 21.5).
> - **Continuous:** Any value within a range (e.g. height = 5.5 ft, 5.55 ft, 5.555 ft).

#### Attribute
A characteristic that **cannot be measured in numbers**, only in quality.
*Examples:* gender, religion, blood group, marital status, honesty.

#### Difference Table

| # | Point | Variable | Attribute |
|---|-------|----------|-----------|
| 1 | Nature | Quantitative | Qualitative |
| 2 | Measurement | In numbers | In words / category |
| 3 | Example | Age, weight | Sex, religion |
| 4 | Used in | Statistical analysis | Theory of attributes |

### Q7. What is Data? Discuss the types of data. &nbsp; `[Marks: 5–10]`

#### Definition
**Data** = the facts and figures collected for a specific purpose.
> Data is the **raw material** of Statistics.

#### Classification of Data

```
                          DATA
                            |
        ----------------------------------------
        |                   |                  |
   By Source           By Nature         By Arrangement
        |                   |                  |
   Primary | Secondary  Quantitative |    Raw  |  Grouped
                          Qualitative
```

#### A. On the basis of Source

| # | Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|------|---------|---------|
| 1 | **Primary data** | Collected by the researcher himself, first-hand | You ask 50 customers in a market |
| 2 | **Secondary data** | Already collected by someone else, used by another person | Bangladesh Bank GDP report |

#### B. On the basis of Nature
- **Quantitative:** In numbers (marks, age, salary)
- **Qualitative:** In quality (gender, religion)

#### C. On the basis of Arrangement
- **Raw data:** Original, not arranged. *Example:* 25, 18, 22, 30, 19
- **Grouped data:** Arranged in classes. *Example:* 0–10, 10–20, 20–30…

#### Difference: Primary vs Secondary Data

| # | Point | Primary | Secondary |
|---|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Collected by | Researcher himself | Someone else |
| 2 | Nature | Original | Second-hand |
| 3 | Cost & Time | More | Less |
| 4 | Reliability | More reliable | Depends on source |
| 5 | Example | Customer survey | BBS website |
