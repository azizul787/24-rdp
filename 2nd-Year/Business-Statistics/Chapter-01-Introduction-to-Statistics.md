# Part A: Easy Explanation (like teaching a class 5 student)

## What is Statistics?

Imagine your school is having a sports day. The headmaster wants to know:
- How many students will join?
- Which class has the most boys, which has the most girls?
- How many medals to order?
- How much food to buy?

To get the answers, every class teacher writes a list and sends it to the headmaster. Now the headmaster has a lot of **numbers**. He puts the numbers in a clean table, finds the totals, draws a chart, and then takes his decision.

This whole work — collecting numbers, arranging them, studying them, and using them to take decisions — is called **STATISTICS**.

So in one easy line:
> **Statistics = working with numbers to take a good decision.**

The word "Statistics" has two meanings:
- **Plural meaning (numbers themselves)** — Example: "Statistics of Bangladesh population is 17 crore." Here statistics means *data*.
- **Singular meaning (the subject)** — Example: "I am studying Statistics in BBA." Here statistics means *the science / subject*.

## Q1. Define Statistics. Discuss the nature / characteristics of Statistics. [Marks: 10]

### Introduction
In modern business, government, and research, no decision can be taken correctly only by guess. Every good decision needs facts, and facts come from numbers. **Statistics** is the science that deals with these numbers. In the field of business administration, statistics has become the most powerful tool for taking right decisions at the right time.

### Origin of the Word
The word "Statistics" came from —
- Latin word **"Status"**
- Italian word **"Statista"**
- German word **"Statistik"**
- French word **"Statistique"**

All these words mean "**political state**." In olden days, kings used statistics to count their people, soldiers, lands and wealth. That is why statistics was first known as the "**science of kings**" or "**science of state**."

### Meaning of Statistics
The word Statistics is used in two senses:

1. **Plural sense (Statistics as Data):** Statistics means the numerical facts collected for any purpose.
   *Example:* Population of Bangladesh, GDP, exam results of NU.

2. **Singular sense (Statistics as Science / Method):** Statistics means the science which deals with **collection, organization, presentation, analysis, and interpretation** of numerical data.

### Definition of Statistics (Important — write at least 2-3 in exam)

1. **Prof. Horace Secrist:**
   "Statistics may be defined as the aggregate of facts affected to a marked extent by multiplicity of causes, numerically expressed, enumerated or estimated according to reasonable standards of accuracy, collected in a systematic manner for a predetermined purpose, and placed in relation to each other."

2. **Croxton and Cowden:**
   "Statistics may be defined as the science which deals with collection, presentation, analysis and interpretation of numerical data."

3. **A. L. Bowley:**
   "Statistics is the science of counting." Again he said, "Statistics is the science of averages."

4. **Prof. R. A. Fisher:**
   "The science of statistics is essentially a branch of applied mathematics and may be regarded as mathematics applied to observational data."

### Nature / Characteristics of Statistics
From the above definitions (specially from Prof. Horace Secrist), we get the following nature or characteristics of Statistics:

1. **Aggregate of facts:** Statistics deals with a group of facts, not a single fact. The mark of one student is *not* statistics, but the marks of all students of a class is statistics.

2. **Numerically expressed:** Statistics deals with only numbers. Qualitative things like beauty, honesty, intelligence are not statistics — unless they are converted into numbers (like marks of an honesty test).

3. **Affected by multiplicity of causes:** Statistical data is affected by many causes together. *Example:* The price of rice depends on production, demand, weather, transport, government policy etc.

4. **Reasonable standard of accuracy:** Data must be collected with reasonable accuracy. 100% accuracy is not always possible, but the data must not mislead the reader.

5. **Collected in a systematic manner:** Data must be collected with proper plan, not in a careless way. Otherwise the result will be wrong.

6. **Collected for a predetermined purpose:** Statistics is always collected for a specific purpose. Without purpose, data has no value.

7. **Placed in relation to each other:** The data must be comparable. We cannot compare the height of students with the price of rice — these are not related.

8. **Enumerated or estimated:** Data may be either fully counted (enumeration) or estimated from a sample (when full counting is not possible).

### Conclusion
From the above discussion, we can say that Statistics is both a **science** and an **art**. It is a science because it follows systematic methods, and it is an art because it uses these methods skillfully to solve real-life business problems. In the modern age, no business, no government, and no research can run a single day without the help of statistics.

## Q2. Discuss the functions of Statistics. [Marks: 10]

### Introduction
Statistics is a very useful science. It helps a businessman, a government officer, a teacher, and a researcher to take the right decision. To do so, statistics performs many important functions in our daily life and in business. The main functions are discussed below.

### Functions of Statistics

1. **Presents facts in a definite form:**
   Statistics presents facts in numerical form, which is more clear and exact than vague statements. Example: "Many students passed" is vague, but "85% students passed" is definite.

2. **Simplifies mass of data:**
   Big and complex data is very hard to understand. Statistics simplifies them by using averages, percentages, ratios, tables, and graphs.

3. **Helps in comparison:**
   Statistics makes comparison easy. We can compare this year's sales with last year's sales, one student's marks with another's, one country's GDP with another country's.

4. **Studies relationship between two or more facts:**
   By using correlation and regression, statistics shows the relationship between two variables. Example: relationship between advertisement cost and sales.

5. **Helps in formulation of policies:**
   Government and business firms make policies based on statistical data. Example: Bangladesh government makes the budget using population, income, and tax data.

6. **Helps in forecasting:**
   By time series analysis, statistics helps to forecast (predict) the future. Example: predicting next year's demand for a product.

7. **Helps in testing hypothesis:**
   In research, statistics is used to test whether a statement (hypothesis) is true or false using methods like t-test, chi-square test, etc.

8. **Enlarges individual knowledge and experience:**
   By using statistics, a single person can know about facts of a whole country or world.

9. **Helps in measuring uncertainty:**
   Through probability theory, statistics helps to measure the chance (risk) of an event happening.

### Conclusion
From the above functions we can clearly understand that statistics is the backbone of every business, every government and every scientific research. Without statistics, decision making would be only a matter of guess.

## Q3. Discuss the importance / scope / uses of Statistics in business. [Marks: 10]

### Introduction
In modern business, every step depends on data. From buying raw materials to selling final products, every decision needs numerical facts. So Statistics has become a must-needed tool for every businessman.

### Importance of Statistics in Business

1. **In Marketing:**
   Statistics helps to know which product is popular, which area has more demand, how much advertisement is needed, and how to fix the price.

2. **In Production / Operation:**
   Statistics helps to control quality, fix the production target, reduce wastage, and check the performance of machines.

3. **In Finance and Accounting:**
   Statistics helps in analyzing profit and loss, ratio analysis, capital budgeting, and forecasting future income.

4. **In Human Resource Management:**
   Statistics helps to measure employee performance, fix salary, find out the rate of absenteeism and labour turnover.

5. **In Banking and Insurance:**
   Banks use statistics to measure the risk of giving a loan. Insurance companies fix the premium using probability and life-table data.

6. **In Investment:**
   Investors use statistics to study market trends, share price movements, and risk before investing money.

7. **In Auditing:**
   Auditors use sampling (a branch of statistics) to check accounts of big companies because checking every transaction is impossible.

8. **In Research and Development (R&D):**
   New products and new ideas are tested by statistical methods before launching in the market.

9. **In Management Control:**
   Top management uses statistics to compare actual performance with the target and take corrective action.

### Conclusion
From the above discussion it is clear that statistics is the **lifeblood of modern business**. Every department of a business — from marketing to finance — depends on statistical data to take wise and profitable decisions.

## Q4. What are the limitations of Statistics? Why do people sometimes distrust statistics? [Marks: 10]

### Introduction
Although Statistics is a very useful science, it is not free from limitations. If statistics is used carelessly or dishonestly, it may give wrong results. That is why some people distrust statistics. The limitations and the reasons of distrust are given below.

### Limitations of Statistics

1. **Statistics does not study individual items:**
   Statistics deals only with aggregates (groups of items). It cannot study a single item separately.

2. **Statistics deals only with quantitative data:**
   Qualitative things like honesty, beauty, intelligence cannot be studied directly by statistics.

3. **Statistical results are true only on average:**
   Statistical conclusions are correct only on the average. They may not be 100% true for every single case.

4. **Statistics can be misused:**
   Statistics is like a useful medicine — used by a good doctor it cures, used by a wrong person it kills. Dishonest people can twist data to mislead.

5. **Statistics requires expert knowledge:**
   Without proper knowledge of statistical methods, the result may be wrong.

6. **Statistics is only one of the methods:**
   Statistics is not the only way to study a problem. Sometimes other methods are also needed.

7. **Statistical laws are not exact:**
   Unlike physics or chemistry, statistical laws are not exact. They are based on probability.

### Reasons for Distrust of Statistics
The famous saying is — *"There are three kinds of lies: lies, damned lies, and statistics."* — by Disraeli. People distrust statistics because of the following reasons:

1. **Use of false data** by dishonest persons.
2. **Wrong interpretation** by people who do not know statistics.
3. **Selection of biased samples** to support a personal opinion.
4. **Use of unfair comparison** (comparing two unrelated things).
5. **Hiding important information** while presenting data.

### Conclusion
So we can say that statistics is not at fault — the fault is with the user. If statistics is used honestly and carefully, it gives the best result. As Prof. W. I. King said, *"Statistics are like clay of which one can make a god or a devil as one pleases."*

## Q5. Distinguish between Population and Sample. [Marks: 5]

### Definition

- **Population (Universe):** The total group of items / persons under study is called population. Example: All students of NU.
- **Sample:** A small part selected from the population is called sample. Example: 100 students selected from NU for a survey.

### Differences

| Point | Population | Sample |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Meaning | Whole group under study | A part of the population |
| Size | Large | Small |
| Symbol of mean | μ (mu) | x̄ (x-bar) |
| Symbol of SD | σ (sigma) | s |
| Cost & Time | More cost, more time | Less cost, less time |
| Method of study | Census method | Sampling method |
| Accuracy | More accurate | Less accurate but reliable |
| Example | All workers of a factory | 50 workers chosen for survey |

### Conclusion
When the population is small, we study population. When the population is very large, we use sample to save time and cost.

## Q6. What is Variable and Attribute? Discuss with examples. [Marks: 5]

### Variable
A **variable** is a characteristic which can be **measured in numbers** and which changes from one item to another.
*Example:* height, weight, age, income, marks.

**Types of Variable:**
- **Discrete variable:** Takes only whole number values. Example: number of students in a class (20, 21, 22 — never 21.5).
- **Continuous variable:** Can take any value within a range. Example: height of a student (5.5 ft, 5.55 ft, 5.555 ft).

### Attribute
An **attribute** is a characteristic which **cannot be measured in numbers** but only described in quality.
*Example:* gender, religion, blood group, marital status, honesty.

### Difference between Variable and Attribute

| Point | Variable | Attribute |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Nature | Quantitative | Qualitative |
| Measurement | In numbers | In words/category |
| Example | Age, weight | Sex, religion |
| Used in | Statistical analysis | Theory of attributes |

## Q7. What is Data? Discuss the types of data. [Marks: 5–10]

### Definition of Data
**Data** means the facts and figures collected for a specific purpose. Data is the **raw material** of statistics.

### Types of Data

#### A. On the basis of source

1. **Primary Data:**
   The data collected by the researcher himself for the first time, directly from the field. It is **original** data.
   *Methods:* direct interview, questionnaire, observation, telephone, mailing.
   *Example:* You go to a market and ask 50 customers about their choice — that is primary data.

2. **Secondary Data:**
   The data which has already been collected by someone else and is being used by another person.
   *Sources:* newspapers, websites, government reports, BBS, journals, books.
   *Example:* You take Bangladesh Bank's published GDP data and use it in your assignment.

#### B. On the basis of nature

1. **Quantitative data:** Data expressed in numbers. Example: marks, age, salary.
2. **Qualitative data:** Data expressed in quality. Example: gender, religion.

#### C. On the basis of arrangement

1. **Raw data / Ungrouped data:** Data in original form, not arranged. Example: 25, 18, 22, 30, 19.
2. **Grouped data:** Data arranged in groups (classes). Example: 0–10, 10–20, 20–30 …

### Difference between Primary and Secondary data

| Point | Primary Data | Secondary Data |
|-------|--------------|----------------|
| Collected by | Researcher himself | Someone else |
| Nature | Original / first-hand | Second-hand |
| Cost & Time | More | Less |
| Reliability | More reliable | Depends on source |
| Example | Survey of customers | Data from BBS website |
